# DEADNET — game interface

The game takes place inside Nora’s late-1990s computer. The player is Adam. An opening outside the desktop establishes why he has the machine and asks him to insert a recovery USB before booting. The USB supplies the casebook; personal applications belong to Nora.

The shell uses MIT 98.css, real gray bevels, navy active title bars, square controls, and actual Windows 98/Outlook Express screenshot references. W95FA is the main interface and story face, generally 17–20 px. VT323 belongs to diagnostics. No modern serif fonts, tiny promotional labels, repeated taglines or generated illustration assets.

A sourced forest photograph fills the desktop. Cream documents and old Messenger history preserve the ordinary life inside it; Nora’s live signal uses a dark green transcript. Licensed Fugue raster icons identify normal applications. The source 2048 touch icon and Phantomicus thumbnail identify those games. The complete sourced Chicago95 White cursor pack supplies pointer, hand, caret, waiting, disabled and directional resize states across the host and all game frames.

Desktop windows drag, resize, minimize, maximize and close. My Computer genuinely navigates Nora’s disk, Documents and the recovery drive. Trash previews/restores two deleted personal drafts. Messenger has contacts and dated history; chats never appear as exported text files. Mail has sender/subject/date and a reading pane. Photos shows real raster images and Nora’s captions. Calendar contains mundane plans. Notepad is editable ordinary notes, rather than instructions.

The casebook saves items from their original applications. The player connects person, motive and means; incorrect theories stay reversible. Supported conclusions produce an authored ending and aftermath. Games are optional and have no evidence-unlock timer. Hocus is removed; five games remain. Their host controls share a dark plum/ash palette. Only Phantomicus displays an on-screen game credit; source notices remain in the distribution.

Power gesture starts the boot sound and ambient atmosphere. A short hushed call, occasional disk/line sounds and the frozen clock carry the unease. Global mute reaches games/voice/ambience. Hidden/background games pause; voice ducks ambience. CRT scanlines default on, with a persistent off switch in Start and the context menu. Reduced motion remains available. Phones use one maximized window and single-tap desktop shortcuts; text stays readable.

Asset provenance and actual historical references are in docs/CREDITS.md and docs/research/VISUAL-REFERENCES.md. The screenshot pack is captured from the running build, with its presentation checkpoints documented separately.
