# Room 317 — experience rebuild

The player is Adam Moore, Nora’s friend. Mara gives him Nora’s computer after the funeral. Her death was dismissed as an accident; his unanswered last message makes the investigation personal. The opening happens outside the desktop: insert the recovery USB, power on, then enter the full browser viewport. This USB supplies the casebook; Nora’s disk supplies only believable personal applications.

## Requirements carried forward
- Period W95FA type, readable controls, real Windows 98 reference screenshots; no modern serif, tiny labels, repetitive taglines or decorative explanations.
- Sourced raster images and credited existing desktop/game code. No generated assets or SVG replacements.
- Forest wallpaper, boot sound, sparse horror sound cues, global mute, reduced motion.
- Five spooky games remain directly discoverable on the desktop, properly credited, paused/muted in background. They are optional; no artificial game-time evidence gates.
- Actual messenger history, mail, photo albums and a calendar. No chat text-file exports, access logs, backup-file treasure hunts, or omniscient evidence dump.
- Ordinary editable Notepad with subtle personal traces, no investigation checklist.
- One short cached ElevenLabs call, existing voice controls and spending guard. No new provider generation calls, per the user’s later instruction.
- Hosted open-weight conversation through existing OpenRouter server adapter, persistent free-account rate limits and explicit scripted fallback. No client-side secrets.
- Preserve Claude’s save validation, autosave error handling, polling, keyboard navigation, game recovery/audio fixes and server guardrails.
- No demo video. Contest article remains unpublished.

## Playable arc
1. Insertion: Mara’s note establishes Adam, Nora, and why this computer matters. USB mounts the casebook.
2. Recognition: messenger opens on a funny ordinary exchange; choosing Oct 31 reveals the unanswered final conversation. Nora’s signal appears on the same desktop.
3. Contradiction: Len’s timestamped bus-home email contradicts Owen’s accusation. A photo caption establishes who had the spare room key. Calendar establishes why Nora was meeting management.
4. Conversation: show Nora a saved item. Grounded AI responds from witnessed evidence and a bounded memory, never invents another puzzle or a solution file.
5. Reconstruction: casebook asks who entered, why, and how. Wrong deductions produce specific uncertainty and remain reversible. Correct supported deductions send the report, release Nora, and deliver a quiet aftermath.

## Architecture boundary
New narrative/content and application surfaces wrap the existing engine, SQLite, provider quota and audio services. Internal evidence IDs remain stable; their visible representations become mail/conversation/photo. Story version advances and old saves are archived, not silently reinterpreted.

## Follow-up decisions

My Computer and Trash Bin are restored as first-class shell apps. The file manager browses Nora’s disk, Documents, the USB casebook and connection utility. Trash previews/restores two ordinary drafts. Hocus :focus is removed entirely. 2048 and Phantomicus use the original upstream icon/thumbnail; only Phantomicus has an on-screen game creator credit. All required licenses remain. CRT scanlines default on with a persistent off switch. A complete Chicago95 White cursor pack covers shell, window descendants, inputs, resize states and game iframes; it replaces the initial single-cursor experiment.
